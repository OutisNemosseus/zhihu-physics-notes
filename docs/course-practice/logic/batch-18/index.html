Week 2 / Module 2 · easy · membership, subsets, nesting, and powersets · 01

Keep labels separate from sets containing labels

The symbols r and s are two distinct labels; neither is a set. Let \(A=\{r,\{s\},\varnothing,\{\varnothing\}\}\). The notation \(\subseteq\) allows equality, and \(\mathcal P(A)\) is the set of all subsets of A.

  1. List the four elements of A without removing any braces, and give \(|A|\). Decide whether each claim is true: \(r\in A\), \(\{r\}\subseteq A\), \(s\in A\), and \(\{s\}\in A\).
  2. Decide whether each claim is true: \(\{\varnothing\}\in A\), \(\{\varnothing\}\subseteq A\), \(\{\{s\}\}\subseteq A\), and \(\{\{s\}\}\in\mathcal P(A)\). Explain which outermost elements you checked in each case.
  3. A proposed rule says, “Whenever a set T is an element of A, T must also be a subset of A.” Test it with \(T=\{s\}\), identifying an element that settles the subset question. Then test \(T=\varnothing\). Does one successful example establish the proposed rule?
中文提示（不含解答）

先数最外层的元素；标签 s 与单元素集合 {s} 是两个不同的对象。

Week 2 / Module 2 · easy · Cartesian products and cardinality · 02

Keep the coordinate order in products

Let \(S=\{-4,2\}\), \(T=\{u,v,w\}\), and \(V=\{0,1\}\). The letters u, v, and w are distinct labels, different from every number displayed here. Ordered pairs and triples retain their coordinate order.

  1. List every element of \(S\times T\) and state its cardinality.
  2. Decide whether each pair belongs to \(S\times T\): \((2,v)\), \((v,2)\), \((-4,u)\), and \((2,2)\). List \(T\times S\), and decide whether it equals \(S\times T\), explaining one difference.
  3. Find \(|S\times T\times V|\). List all triples in this product whose second coordinate is v. How many remaining triples have second coordinate different from v? Check that the two counts add to the full product's cardinality.
中文提示（不含解答）

每个坐标都要检查它自己的来源集合；交换两个坐标通常会改变有序对。

Week 2 / Module 2 · medium · finite operations, complements, and De Morgan · 03

Compare two ways to remove a shared region

The universe is \(U=\{1,2,\ldots,12\}\). Let \(A=\{3,6,9,12\}\), \(B=\{2,3,5,7,11\}\), and \(C=\{1,3,5,9\}\). Every complement is relative to U.

  1. List \((A\cup B)\setminus C\) and \(A\cap(B\cup C)\), and give the cardinality of each.
  2. Compute \((A\cup B)^c\) and \(A^c\cap B^c\) separately. Do they agree? Describe the selected region of a two-set Venn diagram in words, including whether the region inside both circles is selected.
  3. Compute \(L=A\setminus(B\cap C)\) and \(R=(A\setminus B)\cap(A\setminus C)\). If they differ, list every element in exactly one of L and R. Replace only the operation between the two parenthesized differences in R to obtain a formula that always equals L, and explain the repair using membership conditions.
中文提示（不含解答）

“不同时属于 B 和 C”与“既不属于 B 也不属于 C”不同；全集中没有列入任何集合的元素也要考虑。

Week 2 / Module 2 · medium · inclusion-exclusion and region counts · 04

Recount two sign-up lists after a cancellation

A club has exactly 54 members. Let A be the members signed up for the morning session and B the members signed up for the afternoon session. There are 31 members in A, 28 in B, and 7 in neither list. Each member may join both sessions; all complements are relative to these 54 members.

  1. Find the number in \(A\cup B\), in \(A\cap B\), in A only, and in B only. Check that the four disjoint regions add to 54.
  2. Find the number signed up for exactly one session and the number in \((A\cap B)^c\). Explain why those two counts need not agree.
  3. Exactly three members who were in both lists cancel only their morning sign-up; they remain in B. Nobody else changes any membership. Find the new values of \(|A|\), \(|B|\), \(|A\cap B|\), the number in exactly one list, and the number in neither list. Check the new union count by inclusion-exclusion.
中文提示（不含解答）

先把“都不参加”从总数中减掉；取消早场的三人从交集移到仅 B 区域。

Week 2 / Module 2 · medium · real intervals, complements, and indexed operations · 05

Track endpoints through intervals and a family of intervals

The universe is \(\mathbb R\). Let \(A=(-5,2]\), \(B=[-1,6)\), and \(C=(2,8]\). Parentheses exclude an endpoint and square brackets include it. In part (c), n ranges over all positive integers, starting at 1.

  1. Write \(A\cap B\), \(A\cup B\), and \(A\setminus B\) in interval notation. Explain the membership of each finite endpoint in your answers.
  2. Write \((A\cup C)^c\), \(B\setminus(A\cup C)\), and \(A^c\cap C\) in interval notation, using \(\varnothing\) when appropriate. Explain what happens at the meeting point 2.
  3. For \(I_n=[-2-1/n,\,5-1/n)\), find \(\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}I_n\) and \(\bigcap_{n=1}^{\infty}I_n\). Justify the proposed intervals, and explicitly check the membership or exclusion of -3, -2, 4, and 5 in each result. A point is in the union if it is in at least one \(I_n\), and in the intersection if it is in every \(I_n\).
中文提示（不含解答）

区间族的交集要求同一点通过每一个 n；处理极限端点时，直接检查它是否属于各个区间。

Week 2 / Module 2 · medium · powerset operations and counterexamples · 06

Find subsets created by joining two sets

Let \(A=\{j,k\}\) and \(B=\{k,\ell\}\), where j, k, and \(\ell\) are three distinct labels. The powerset \(\mathcal P(X)\) contains every subset of X, including the empty set. All operations on powersets treat whole subsets as elements.

  1. List \(\mathcal P(A)\), \(\mathcal P(B)\), and \(\mathcal P(A\cap B)\). Compare \(\mathcal P(A\cap B)\) with \(\mathcal P(A)\cap\mathcal P(B)\), explaining why a subset in one must be in the other.
  2. List \(\mathcal P(A\cup B)\setminus(\mathcal P(A)\cup\mathcal P(B))\). Use your list to decide whether taking a powerset always distributes over union. Explain what each listed subset contains that prevents it from belonging to either smaller powerset.
  3. List both \(\mathcal P(A\setminus B)\) and \(\mathcal P(A)\setminus\mathcal P(B)\). Is either one a subset of the other? For each failed direction, give a specific element of the left side that is missing from the right side, preserving every layer of braces.
中文提示（不含解答）

比较幂集时，一个“元素”本身就是一个集合；空集也必须单独检查。

Week 2 / Module 2 · medium · finite injectivity, surjectivity, and single-entry changes · 07

Change a codomain or repair one missing output

The function \(f:D\to E\) has domain \(D=\{-4,-2,0,2,4,6\}\) and codomain \(E=\{10,12,14,16,18\}\). Its complete table is:

x-4-20246
f(x)121414181012
  1. List the range \(f(D)\), every unordered pair of distinct inputs with equal outputs, and every element of E with no preimage. Decide whether f is injective, surjective, and bijective.
  2. Keep the same inputs and output values, but declare the codomain to be \(E'=f(D)\). Reevaluate injectivity, surjectivity, and bijectivity. Explain exactly which property is affected by this codomain change.
  3. Return to the original codomain E. You may replace the output in exactly one table entry by another element of E. Find every such one-entry change that makes the function surjective. Explain why your list is complete and whether any function from this six-element D to the five-element E can be injective.
中文提示（不含解答）

满射必须覆盖声明的整个陪域；补上缺失输出时，也要防止丢掉原来只出现一次的输出。

Week 2 / Module 2 · medium · rational bijections, inverse proof errors, and composition · 08

Check a rational affine inverse and the order of composition

Both functions have domain and codomain \(\mathbb Q\), the rational numbers:

\(f(x)=\dfrac{7x-9}{4},\qquad g(x)=3x+2.\)

  1. Prove that f is injective by starting with \(f(a)=f(b)\), where \(a,b\in\mathbb Q\). To test surjectivity, let \(y\in\mathbb Q\) be arbitrary and solve \(f(x)=y\) for x. Explain why your chosen x belongs to the stated domain, and decide whether f is bijective.
  2. A proposed inverse is \(h(y)=(4y-9)/7\). Compute \(f(h(y))\) and give a rational y showing why this is not an inverse. Correct the formula for \(f^{-1}\) and verify both \(f(f^{-1}(y))=y\) and \(f^{-1}(f(x))=x\) on their stated rational domains.
  3. Compute formulas for \((f\circ g)(x)\) and \((g\circ f)(x)\), including their domains and codomains. Decide whether these two functions are equal, using the input 0 as a check. For each composition, solve its output equation for x to decide whether it is bijective on \(\mathbb Q\).

The convention is \((f\circ g)(x)=f(g(x))\). A surjectivity argument must work for every rational target y, not merely one example.

中文提示（不含解答）

满射证明要从任意目标 y 反解输入，并检查输入仍是有理数；复合时先做靠右的函数。

Week 2 / Module 2 · synthesis · finite composition, complete cases, and minimal repair · 09

Complete a first map so the composite is a bijection

Let \(X=\{a,b,c\}\), \(Y=\{u,v,w,t\}\), and \(Z=\{0,1,2\}\), with all letters distinct labels. The complete function \(g:Y\to Z\) is \(g(u)=0\), \(g(v)=1\), \(g(w)=1\), and \(g(t)=2\). A function \(f:X\to Y\) has \(f(a)=u\); the values \(f(b)\) and \(f(c)\) are not yet chosen. The composite is \(h=g\circ f:X\to Z\).

  1. Find every ordered pair \((f(b),f(c))\) for which h is bijective, and give the corresponding output triple \((h(a),h(b),h(c))\). Explain why the list is complete. For every successful completion, decide whether f is injective and whether it is surjective onto Y; also classify the fixed function g.
  2. Now impose \(f(b)=w\) and retain the requirement that h is bijective. Determine f completely, compute h, and list the inverse map \(h^{-1}:Z\to X\). Explain why a bijective composite here does not make each of f and g bijective.
  3. Starting from the completed maps in part (b), change only \(g(w)\) from 1 to 2. Compute the new composite and identify its repeated and missing outputs. Keep this changed g and keep \(f(a)=u\). Find every way to restore a bijective composite by changing exactly one of \(f(b)\) or \(f(c)\), and justify completeness.
中文提示（不含解答）

复合函数需要输出 0、1、2 各一次；先按 g 的输出把 Y 中的候选值分组，再选择 f 的值。

Week 2 / Module 2 · synthesis · sets–logic bridge, quantified negation, and minimal edits · 10

Turn two membership rules into bounds on one set

The universe is \(U=\{a,b,c,d,e,f,g,h\}\). Fix \(A=\{a,c,d,g\}\) and \(B=\{b,c,e,g\}\). Initially \(C=\{a,c,f\}\). For \(x\in U\), let P(x), Q(x), and R(x) mean respectively \(x\in A\), \(x\in B\), and \(x\in C\). Two rules are required:

  • S: Every element belonging to exactly one of A and B belongs to C.
  • T: Every element belonging to C belongs to at least one of A and B.
  1. Translate S and T using \(\forall,\to,\neg,\land,\lor\) and the displayed predicates. Negate each rule, placing negations directly on predicates and using an existential quantifier. Using the original C, list every counterexample to each rule and decide whether each rule holds.
  2. Keep A and B fixed and allow C to be any subset of U. Find a required lower set L and an allowed upper set H such that both rules hold exactly when \(L\subseteq C\subseteq H\). List L and H, and then enumerate every permitted C. Explain why no other C works.
  3. Return to the original \(C=\{a,c,f\}\). One edit either adds one previously absent element of U to C or removes one current element from C; A, B, and U stay fixed. Find the minimum number of edits that makes both rules hold, and list every resulting C obtainable with that minimum. Explain why each required addition or removal is unavoidable.

“At least one” includes membership in both sets. A counterexample to a universal implication makes its antecedent true and its consequent false.

中文提示（不含解答）

第一条规定必须加入哪些元素，第二条限制允许保留哪些元素；A 与 B 的共同元素可能保留选择空间。
