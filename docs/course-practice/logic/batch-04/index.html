Module 1 · easy · proposition recognition and binding · 01

Separate uncertainty from open variables

A sealed card shows exactly one fixed integer from \(D=\{-1,0,1\}\). You do not know which integer it shows. In the statements below, an x without a quantifier is an unassigned variable whose domain is D.

  1. The card shows 0.
  2. \(x^2=x\).
  3. \(\forall x\in D\;(x^2\ge0)\).
  4. \(\exists x\in D\;(x^2=2)\).
  5. Which number is on the card?

For each item, decide whether it is a proposition as written. For each proposition, give its truth value if the information determines it; otherwise explain why the truth value cannot be inferred from the information provided. Explain why each non-proposition fails to be a proposition.

Finally, close item 2 in two different ways, once with a universal quantifier and once with an existential quantifier over D. Determine the truth value of each resulting proposition.

中文提示（不含解答）

区分“客观上有确定真假，但你不知道”与“自由变量尚未确定”；量词可以把开放式变成命题。

Module 1 · easy · inclusive or and exclusive choice · 02

Read at most one precisely

Let p mean “Output channel P is selected” and q mean “Output channel Q is selected.” Consider these three requirements:

  • A: At most one of the two channels is selected.
  • B: Exactly one of the two channels is selected.
  • C: Neither channel is selected.
  1. Translate A, B, and C using only p, q, \(\neg\), \(\land\), and \(\lor\).
  2. For each requirement, list every allowed ordered pair (p, q). Use the order TT, TF, FT, FF when listing or checking possibilities.
  3. Are A and B equivalent? Explain what happens when neither channel is selected.
  4. Give an additional requirement that can be joined to A with “and” to produce a statement equivalent to B.

All uses of \(\lor\) are inclusive.

中文提示（不含解答）

“至多”允许少于上限；“恰好”还要满足下限。先检查零个、一个和两个被选中的情况。

Module 1 · medium · truth tables and classification · 03

Test a three-input exclusive-or claim

For propositions U and V, define \(U\oplus V\) to be true exactly when one of U and V is true and the other is false. Let

\(A=(p\oplus q)\oplus r\)

and

\(B=(p\land\neg q\land\neg r)\lor(\neg p\land q\land\neg r)\lor(\neg p\land\neg q\land r).\)

  1. Construct a truth table with columns p, q, r, \(p\oplus q\), A, B, and \(A\leftrightarrow B\). Use the row order TTT, TTF, TFT, TFF, FTT, FTF, FFT, FFF.
  2. A designer says that A means “Exactly one of p, q, and r is true.” Test that interpretation by listing every row on which A and B differ.
  3. Classify \(A\leftrightarrow B\), \(A\to B\), and \(B\to A\) as tautologies, contradictions, or contingencies.
  4. Describe in words exactly how many of p, q, and r may be true when A is true.
Reference / 必要约定与公式\(U\oplus V\equiv(U\lor V)\land\neg(U\land V)\).
中文提示（不含解答）

先计算括号中的二元异或；不要把二元连接词的直觉直接推广到三个命题。

Module 1 · medium · equivalence by cases · 04

Analyze a conditional selector

A selector uses q when p is true and r when p is false. Two proposed formulas for its output are

\(E=(p\land q)\lor(\neg p\land r),\qquad F=(p\to q)\land(\neg p\to r).\)

  1. Rewrite F without implication symbols.
  2. When p is true, simplify E and F separately. Repeat when p is false. Use these two cases to decide whether E and F are logically equivalent.
  3. A proposed simplification replaces E with \(q\lor r\). Is that replacement always equivalent? If not, give every assignment to p, q, and r on which the two expressions differ.
  4. Determine whether each conditional \(E\to(q\lor r)\) and \((q\lor r)\to E\) is a tautology. Explain the difference between the two directions.

Use inclusive “or.” You may use case analysis on truth values; no formal proof system is required.

Reference / 必要约定与公式\(U\to V\equiv\neg U\lor V\).
中文提示（不含解答）

分别固定 p 为真、为假，再化简两个式子；检查等价时也要检查未被选中的分支。

Module 1 · medium · converse inverse and contrapositive · 05

Distinguish logical form from this example

Both x and y range independently over exactly \(D=\{-2,-1,0,1,2\}\). Consider the universal statement:

“For every x and y in D, if \(xy=0\), then \(x=0\) or \(y=0\).”

  1. Write the converse, inverse, and contrapositive as complete universally quantified statements over the same domain. When negating “x = 0 or y = 0,” move the negations onto the individual equalities.
  2. Determine the truth value of all four statements. Give a short arithmetic explanation for each true statement or an allowed ordered pair (x, y) as a counterexample for each false statement.
  3. Identify the pairs that are equivalent solely because of their logical form, independent of these particular arithmetic predicates.
  4. Does your result in part 2 establish that every implication is logically equivalent to its converse? Test that general claim by giving truth values for unrelated propositions p and q that distinguish \(p\to q\) from \(q\to p\), or explain why no such values exist.

“Or” is inclusive, and ordered pairs may have equal coordinates.

中文提示（不含解答）

先用两个字母代表前件和后件来变换方向；具体谓词恰好具有的性质，与对任意命题成立的逻辑规律不同。

Module 1 · medium · semantic argument validity · 06

Combine two overlapping reports

Let p mean “The left sensor is active,” q mean “The backup indicator is lit,” and r mean “The warning indicator is lit.” The only premises are

\(p\lor q,\qquad\neg p\lor r.\)

  1. Check all eight assignments to p, q, and r, and list every assignment that makes both premises true. Use the order TTT, TTF, TFT, TFF, FTT, FTF, FFT, FFF.
  2. Does \(q\lor r\) follow from the premises? Justify using the compatible assignments.
  3. Does \(q\land r\) follow? If it does not, list every compatible assignment that makes this proposed conclusion false.
  4. A reliable additional observation says q is false. Determine what the premises and this observation force about p and r.

All uses of “or” are inclusive. No relationship between the sensors and indicators is assumed beyond the stated premises.

Reference / 必要约定与公式An argument is valid exactly when no assignment makes every premise true and its conclusion false.
中文提示（不含解答）

只保留两个前提同时为真的行；“至少一个”与“两个都”需要分别检验。

Module 1 · medium · quantifier scope and implication · 07

Check the scope of a universal condition

The domain is exactly \(D=\{1,2,3\}\). Initially, P(x) means “x is odd,” and Q(x) means “x is less than 3.” Compare

\(L=\forall x\in D\,[P(x)\to Q(x)]\)

with

\(G=(\forall x\in D\,P(x))\to(\forall x\in D\,Q(x)).\)

  1. Translate L and G into English while preserving their different quantifier scopes.
  2. Using the initial definitions of P and Q, determine the truth value of L and G. Show the relevant predicate values.
  3. Now allow P and Q to be arbitrary predicates on this same three-element domain. Must \(L\to G\) always be true? Must \(G\to L\) always be true? For each direction, either explain why it always holds or give complete P and Q values on D that make it false.

A complete counterexample specifies both predicates at all three domain elements. You may reason directly from the meanings of “every” and “if”; no formal proof system is required.

中文提示（不含解答）

先确定条件句是在量词里面还是外面；评估外层条件句时，前件本身也是一个完整命题。

Module 1 · medium · uniqueness and nested quantifier negation · 08

Negate an exactly-one assignment policy

The worker domain is \(W=\{A,B,C\}\), and the task domain is \(T=\{1,2\}\). Let R(w,t) mean “worker w is assigned to task t.” Both w and v range over W. Consider

\(U=\forall t\in T\,\exists w\in W\,[R(w,t)\land\forall v\in W\,(R(v,t)\to v=w)].\)

  1. Translate U into English. Explain the role of both parts inside the square brackets.
  2. Negate U. Remove every implication and move every negation directly onto an occurrence of R; write the negation of \(v=w\) as \(v\ne w\).
  3. Translate your negation into English, describing all the ways the assignment policy can fail.
  4. The complete assignment table is given below. Evaluate U and the weaker claim \(V=\forall t\in T\,\exists w\in W\,R(w,t)\). Identify the task and workers that justify any difference between their truth values.
Worker / task12
ATF
BTF
CFT

Assignments outside these domains are irrelevant, and the two quantified worker variables may have the same value.

Module 1 · synthesis · universal implications and guaranteed verification · 09

Choose which hidden faces to inspect

There are exactly four cards, \(C=\{c_1,c_2,c_3,c_4\}\). Each card has one letter side, showing either A or B, and one number side, showing either 1 or 2. One side of each card is visible:

CardVisible face
c1A
c2B
c31
c42

Cards may repeat the same letter or number; there are no counting restrictions or other relationships between cards. Let P(c) mean “card c has letter A,” and Q(c) mean “card c has number 1.” The printed rule says, “Every card with letter A has number 1.”

  1. Write the rule with a quantifier. Write its negation with negations only on predicates, and write its contrapositive as a universally quantified English statement.
  2. For each visible card, decide whether any allowed hidden face would make that card violate the rule. List every such violating hidden face, or explain why none is possible.
  3. Before seeing any hidden face, choose a fixed set of cards to turn over. Find the smallest set that guarantees you can determine whether the rule holds for all four cards, whatever the hidden faces turn out to be. Justify both the sufficiency of your choice and the necessity of every card in it.
  4. Repeat part 3 if the printed rule instead says, “A card has letter A if and only if it has number 1.” State how each visible card could now reveal a violation.

Turning a card reveals only that card’s hidden face. An observation may disprove a rule immediately, but your chosen fixed inspection set must guarantee a decision for every allowed hidden configuration.

中文提示（不含解答）

寻找能使“前件真、后件假”的隐藏面；保证检验成功，需要考虑每个尚未查看的面仍可能怎样变化。

Module 1 · synthesis · quantified predicates and complete model construction · 10

Build a counterexample with separate witnesses

The domain is exactly \(D=\{A,B,C\}\). Let R(x,y) mean “person x sends a report to person y.” Reports to oneself are disallowed, so \(R(A,A)\), \(R(B,B)\), and \(R(C,C)\) are all false. The six remaining truth values are unrestricted. Compare

\(P=\forall x\in D\,\exists y\in D\,[R(x,y)\land R(y,x)]\)

and

\(Q=[\forall x\in D\,\exists y\in D\,R(x,y)]\land[\forall x\in D\,\exists y\in D\,R(y,x)].\)

  1. Translate P and Q into English. Explain which occurrences of y must refer to the same witness and which may use different witnesses.
  2. Across all complete truth tables satisfying the fixed false diagonal, decide whether P always implies Q and whether Q always implies P. For a direction that fails, give a complete three-by-three counterexample table; for a direction that holds, justify it from the meaning of the quantifiers.
  3. Negate P, moving each negation directly onto R, and translate the result into English.
  4. Among all tables in which Q is true and P is false, find the minimum possible number of T entries. Give every complete table achieving that minimum, with rows and columns both ordered A, B, C. Explain why fewer T entries cannot work and why your list of minimum tables is complete.

A table entry in row x and column y is R(x,y). There are no assumptions of reciprocity: R(x,y) and R(y,x) may differ. Reason only with predicate values and quantifiers; no graph terminology is needed.

中文提示（不含解答）

分开检查每个人的发送见证与接收见证；构造最小表格时，先给出必要数量下界，再排除遗漏的排列。
