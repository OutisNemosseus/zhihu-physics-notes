# Is the Pseudogap a Universal Phenomenon?

> **Original Chinese title:** 赝能隙是不是普遍现象？  
> **Original Chinese version:** [Zhihu](https://www.zhihu.com/question/1611552070/answer/2067982461733167657)

There are several mechanisms that can produce a pseudogap.

A pseudogap is not unique to high-\(T_c\) superconductors. It can also appear in comparatively conventional superconducting systems, such as sufficiently two-dimensional TiN films.

One useful picture is the following:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Cooper pairing can begin before global phase coherence is established.}
}
\]

In this regime, electrons may already form Cooper pairs, while the phases of those pairs are not yet globally coherent.

That creates an intermediate state in which the single-particle spectrum is already suppressed near the Fermi level, even though true long-range superconducting coherence has not yet developed.

For a more three-dimensional conventional superconductor, pairing and phase coherence can occur at nearly the same temperature, so this intermediate regime may become very small or disappear.

This should not be interpreted as the unique explanation of every pseudogap. In more complicated materials, several different mechanisms can generate pseudogap-like behavior. Preformed pairing is only one possible mechanism.

A related example appears in ultrathin Pb films. As the system crosses over from two-dimensional toward three-dimensional behavior, the phase stiffness increases and the pseudogap can gradually disappear.
